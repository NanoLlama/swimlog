1The problem the layout solves

Plate position is not neutral, and neither is plate membership.

A PhIP-Seq run spreads a cohort across several 96-well plates. Each plate is processed as a batch: its own bead lot, its own thermocycler position, its own operator-hours. Systematic differences between plates are real and expected.

That only becomes a problem when plate membership lines up with something biological. If every Lupus sample sits on plate 1 and every healthy control on plate 4, then "Lupus versus healthy" and "plate 1 versus plate 4" are the same contrast, measured once. No amount of downstream statistics separates them, because the design never let them vary independently. The layout is where that is won or lost — after the plates are pipetted it is too late.

The planner's job is therefore to make every plate a scale model of the run: each cohort present on each plate in roughly its run-wide proportion. Two constraints pull against that, and both are honoured:

  • The bench needs contiguity. A layout that scatters a cohort across a plate is slower to pipette by hand and easier to get wrong. Samples are therefore dealt as contiguous runs — the consecutive block of tubes you pick up from the rack — rather than as a scattered subset.
  • Subjects should not be split. A subject with a serum and a CSF draw, or two longitudinal draws, is kept whole on one plate so that a within-subject comparison is never also a between-plate comparison.

2Terms used precisely

Five words that mean something specific here.

sample
One row of the manifest — one tube, identified by tiu_id.
unit
What the dealer actually moves: one sample, or all of a subject's samples when keep subjects together is on. Units are indivisible — this is the single biggest source of imperfect balance, and section 11 says why.
group
The stratification key — cohort by default, or diagnosis, sample type, sex, or the manifest's own dither_group column. This is the variable the planner balances.
chunk
A set of replicates adjacent wells that one sample occupies — normally a side-by-side pair such as A1+A2. Chunks are the unit of plate capacity; a plate's capacity is counted in chunks, not wells.
window
A consecutive block of plates across which each group is balanced — the "dither across N plates" setting. A window of 4 balances each cohort across plates 1–4, then across 5–8.

3What gets dealt, and what is held back

The planner deliberately refuses to place several categories of sample.

Of the manifest, only these are dealt automatically:

  • Serum, CSF and plasma — after type normalisation, so "Serum ", Plasma (EDTA) and CSF (post-LP) all qualify.

Everything else is held back, each for its own reason:

  • Controls and mAbs are never auto-filled. They belong in the reserved control area, which is placed by template rather than dealt — their position is a deliberate choice, not a consequence of sample count.
  • Bridging samples (marked repeat_across_plates) are placed before the pool competes for wells, once per target plate, so the batch-correction anchor is never crowded out by ordinary samples.
  • Samples already on a plate are skipped, so a hand-placed layout is preserved and can be completed by autofill rather than overwritten.
  • Samples excluded at the volume pre-flight are not planned at all; those reduced to a singlet are planned normally but written into one well of their chunk, leaving the partner deliberately empty.

Held-back samples still consume capacity. A bridge on every plate costs one chunk per plate, and the plate-count calculation subtracts that before deciding how many plates the run needs — otherwise the last plate silently overflows.

4The pipeline

Seven stages, each of which can only narrow what the next one sees.

5Units, groups and order

Units are built first, and never split

With keep subjects together on, every sample sharing a subject_id becomes one unit. A sample with no subject id becomes its own unit. When sample types are being kept off the same plate, the key becomes subject_id + sample_type — a subject with both a serum and a CSF draw cannot be whole on one plate and typed apart, so the tool holds the subject together within each type and says so in the preview.

In the reference run, 200 eligible samples collapse to 102 units. The dealer from here on moves units, not samples, which is what makes a subject's draws inseparable.

Groups are queued in manifest order

Units are bucketed by the stratification key. The group order is first appearance in the manifest — not alphabetical, not by size — so "cohort A, then cohort B" on the plate means what it says in the spreadsheet. Within a group, units keep manifest order unless randomise sample order is on, in which case they are shuffled with the seeded generator.

Shuffling within a group is what turns each plate's share of a cohort from a consecutive block of the spreadsheet into a random subset of it. Without it, plate 1 gets the first-enrolled patients and plate 5 the last — which matters if enrolment order tracks anything, such as season, site, or disease stage at recruitment.

6Windows and quotas

This is the stage that produces the balance.

How many plates the run uses

Dithering across more plates than the samples need would leave half-empty plates behind, so the planner first walks the plate list accumulating capacity — minus each plate's bridge load — until the demand is met. Those become the active plates; anything beyond is held as reserve, receives no quota, and is only reached by genuine overflow. In the reference run, 102 units need 5 of the 8 available plates.

Quota by largest remainder

The active plates are cut into windows of the requested size (4 by default, giving windows [1,2,3,4] and [5]). Each group's total demand is then divided across the windows in proportion to window capacity, using largest-remainder apportionment — the same method used to allocate legislative seats, chosen because it distributes whole indivisible things without accumulating rounding drift:

7Dealing a group across a window

Where the theory meets indivisible units.

Within a window the groups are dealt one after another in manifest order — all of cohort A, then all of cohort B — so each plate receives a solid run of each cohort. The quota is expressed in wells, but units are placed whole, so a plate can only land exactly on target when the target happens to be reachable from the unit sizes to hand. Two corrections stop that error from compounding down the window:

Overflow is packed, not spread

Units a window could not take flow into the next window. Anything still in hand at the end is placed on the first plate with room, rather than apportioned across all remaining capacity. Spreading a six-well remainder evenly is technically the most even thing to do and the worst thing to hand a lab: it leaves two wells stranded on plate 8 and a whole plate that has to be run for them. Packing keeps the tail compact.

8From units to wells

Which plate is now settled; this stage only decides where on it.

Each plate's wells are enumerated in a canonical slot order set by the replicate layout — for side-by-side duplicates filling downward, that is A1, A2, B1, B2 … H1, H2, then the next column pair. The order is cut into chunks of replicates wells, and a chunk is usable only if every well in it is free and outside the control area.

That all-or-nothing test is what guarantees replicates are physically adjacent, even on a plate someone has already part-filled by hand. A chunk with one well taken is discarded rather than half-used — its free wells are reported as stranded in the preview rather than silently filled, because using them would put two different samples in one duplicate pair.

Ordering within the plate

Units are sorted before being written to chunks, by four keys in order:

  1. Pinned first — bridges hold their fixed chunk.
  2. Sample type — a plate must never interleave preps, since serum and CSF are handled differently at the bench.
  3. Group rank — the block order, which is where the three well placement options differ.
  4. Manifest order, then label, as a deterministic tiebreak.

The group rank is fixed under ordered, a seeded per-plate permutation under blocks shuffled, and under edge-balanced is chosen by scoring 60 seeded candidate orderings against each plate's actual edge-well exposure and keeping the best. The plain manifest order is always among the candidates, so balancing can never do worse than not balancing.

9Reproducibility

Same seed, same inputs, same options — same layout, byte for byte.

All randomness comes from a seeded mulberry32 generator over an FNV-style hash of the seed string. No use of Math.random() anywhere in the planner. Independent decisions draw from independently derived streams, so that changing one does not shift the others:

StreamDerived fromControls
mainseedsample-order shuffle, scatter well order
blocksseed|blocks|platewhich column block each group gets
edgeseed|edge|plateedge-balance candidate orderings
bridgeseed|bridge|platerotated bridge positions
controlseed|ctl|platerotated control-block offset

The Design Log records the seed alongside the designer version, the build commit, the state schema version, and a fingerprint of the manifest's tiu_id order. Those five together are what let a reader confirm they are regenerating from identical inputs with identical code, rather than from something that merely looks the same. Re-opening an exported workbook restores the seed and every option from a hidden state sheet, so a design is reproducible from the file itself and not only from someone's notes.

Position

Contiguous cohort blocks balance cohort across plates but confound it with column position. Edge exposure — the share of a group's wells in row A/H or column 1/12 — under the default ordering runs from 16.0% to 43.6% against a run-wide 32.5%, a spread of 16.5 pp. Selecting edge-balanced block order brings the worst deviation to 3.1 pp without moving any sample to a different plate and without breaking block contiguity.

11What the algorithm does not do

Limits worth stating before a layout is defended in a methods section.

  • Stratification is single-factor. One key is balanced; everything else is left to chance and merely reported. There is no multi-factor optimiser.
  • Quotas are only reachable through whole units. A subject contributing 4 wells cannot half-satisfy a target of 2. Deviations of one unit are structural, not a defect — they are the price of never splitting a subject.
  • Stratifying on a two-level covariate buys less than expected. On the reference manifest, stratifying on sex leaves sex worse balanced (8.5 pp) than stratifying on cohort does (6.5 pp), because whole-subject units constrain the achievable split more than the choice of key does. Check the diagnostics rather than assuming.
  • A run can end with a small remainder plate. Packing keeps it to one, but a four-well tail is possible and that plate is not a meaningful unit for balance — the diagnostics exclude plates under 8 wells from the headline deviation for exactly that reason.
  • Nothing here randomises across plates independently of the blocks. Position and cohort are decoupled only to the degree the chosen well-placement option allows.

12How the behaviour is verified

Every claim above is pinned by an automated test; the suite runs 159 tests over the planner and exports. Three layers:

  • Golden layouts. Four reference configurations are stored well-by-well. Any change to the planner that alters them fails the suite and has to be justified deliberately, rather than slipping through as an unnoticed side effect.
  • Invariants, asserted over the golden configurations and 100 fuzzed manifests (10–2000 samples, 1–15 groups, 1–20 plates): every placed sample occupies exactly replicates wells; replicate wells are adjacent under the selected layout; no reserved well is ever written by autofill; no subject is split when kept together; placed + unplaced + stranded accounts for every eligible sample; no well is written twice; same seed gives an identical plan and a different seed does not; controls never enter a plan.
  • Validate layout, in the tool itself, runs the same rules against the live design including anything placed or edited by hand, and names the offending wells rather than returning a verdict.

To reproduce a layout from an exported workbook: open it in the designer, which restores the seed, window, grouping and every option from the hidden state sheet, then re-run autofill on an empty plate set. The Design Log in the export carries the same parameters in readable form.
